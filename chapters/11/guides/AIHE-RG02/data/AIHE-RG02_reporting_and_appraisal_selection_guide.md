# AIHE-RG02 — Reporting and Appraisal Tool Selection Guide

**Aligned chapters:** 7, 8, and 11 
**Primary use:** match study design and evidence stage to the appropriate reporting or appraisal instrument.

## Core distinction

A reporting guideline asks whether essential information has been reported. An appraisal instrument asks whether evidence is at risk of bias, is applicable, or supports confidence. Neither function by itself determines whether a technology should be adopted. Adoption also requires local validity, workflow utility, economic analysis, equity assessment, governance, and continuing monitoring.

## Selection table

| Evidence or study type | Reporting instrument | Appraisal or complementary approach | Current status and role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prediction model development or validation | TRIPOD+AI | PROBAST+AI | Published AI-updated reporting and appraisal instruments |
| Diagnostic accuracy study | STARD-AI | Diagnostic bias, applicability, reference-standard, and local-validity appraisal | Published AI-specific reporting guideline; use the current corrected version |
| Early live clinical evaluation of AI decision support | DECIDE-AI | Local safety, human-factors, workflow, and implementation appraisal | Published reporting guideline for early-stage live evaluation, not a regulatory approval |
| AI intervention trial protocol | SPIRIT-AI | Protocol feasibility, intervention specification, data and human–AI interaction review | Published consensus extension |
| AI intervention trial report | CONSORT-AI | Risk-of-bias, applicability, subgroup, implementation, and economic appraisal | Published consensus extension |
| Economic evaluation | CHEERS 2022 and CHEERS-AI | Economic-method, model-validation, uncertainty, budget-impact, distributional, and transferability appraisal | Published reporting guidance; not a methodological quality score |
| Systematic review | PRISMA 2020 | Appropriate risk-of-bias tools, certainty assessment, and synthesis appraisal | Published reporting guideline |
| Qualitative evidence synthesis | Appropriate review reporting guidance | GRADE-CERQual for confidence in synthesis findings | Established appraisal approach for qualitative synthesis findings |
| Local silent-mode or prospective evaluation | DECIDE-AI concepts plus a local protocol | Predefined thresholds, human–AI performance, workflow, equity, economic, and incident review | Institutional evidence process; requires local governance and decision criteria |
| Generative or multimodal AI evaluation | Task-specific transparent reporting and versioned test assets | Factuality, omission, harmful output, provenance, review burden, subgroup, robustness, and lifecycle appraisal | Rapidly developing area; avoid claiming a single universal standard |

## Use instructions

1. Specify the intended use and decision consequence.
2. Identify the study design and evidence stage.
3. Select the reporting guideline that matches that design.
4. Select separate appraisal methods for bias, applicability, certainty, local validity, economic value, and implementation.
5. Record the exact version, correction, extension, and evidence cut-off.
6. Document gaps that the selected instrument does not address.
