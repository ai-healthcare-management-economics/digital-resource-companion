# AIHE-RG05 — Scenario Planning and Real-Options Worksheet

**Aligned chapters:** 1, 6–9, 12, and 15 
**Primary use:** test whether a decision remains defensible across plausible futures and whether staged commitments preserve useful options.

## Suggested scenarios

| Scenario | Central uncertainty | Questions for the decision-maker | Flexible response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulation-intensive environment | Evidence, documentation, oversight, and change-control requirements increase | Can the institution produce the required evidence and retain auditability without making the intervention unaffordable? | Pilot, modular contract, evidence milestones, update rights, pause clause |
| Vendor-platform dependence | Critical data, workflows, or models become tied to one supplier | What are the consequences of price escalation, service withdrawal, acquisition, or incompatible updates? | Portability, escrow where appropriate, open standards, exit assistance, dual sourcing |
| Cross-institutional data space | Governed data sharing becomes easier | Does larger-scale evidence improve representativeness enough to justify coordination, semantic, privacy, and governance cost? | Federated or secure-environment pilot, harmonization milestones, conditional expansion |
| Agentic workflow support | AI executes or coordinates multiple actions | Which steps require approval, monitoring, rollback, or prohibition? | Constrained sandbox, stepwise authorization, transaction limits, kill switch, audit logs |
| Resource-constrained health system | Capital, workforce, data, and assurance capacity remain limited | Which investments create the greatest feasible value, and which AI proposals should be deferred? | Non-AI comparator, shared service, small reversible pilot, capability investment first |
| Trust or safety shock | A serious incident changes public and professional confidence | Can the institution communicate, investigate, restrict, and learn without concealing uncertainty? | Incident plan, independent review, temporary suspension, transparent restart criteria |

## Real-options logic

A pilot has value when it purchases information that can change a later decision. Modularity preserves the option to replace components. An exit clause preserves the option to stop. A delayed investment may preserve capital while evidence matures. These options are not automatically beneficial: they should be valued against delay, duplication, transition, and learning costs.
