# AIHE-RG01 — Framework Status and Selection Guide

**Aligned chapters:** 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12 
**Primary use:** distinguish instruments by authority, evidentiary role, and decision function before selecting them.

## Why this guide is needed

Healthcare AI literature often places legislation, standards, reporting guidance, appraisal instruments, implementation frameworks, and locally proposed decision aids in one undifferentiated list. They are not interchangeable. A legal instrument may create binding duties in a jurisdiction; a standard may specify an auditable management system; a reporting guideline may improve the completeness of a manuscript; an appraisal tool may structure critical assessment; an implementation framework may explain context or outcomes; and an author-developed framework may integrate several domains without having external validation.

## Selection rules

1. Begin with the decision and jurisdiction, not with a familiar acronym.
2. Establish whether the instrument is binding law, a published standard, voluntary guidance, a reporting guideline, an appraisal tool, an implementation framework, an interoperability specification, or a proposed book-specific aid.
3. Use the instrument only for the function it was designed to perform.
4. Do not infer safety, effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, fairness, or regulatory conformity from checklist completion.
5. Record edition, pub date, update date, applicable jurisdiction, and evidence cut-off.
6. Treat author-developed frameworks in the book as proposed decision-support structures requiring independent validation.

## Reference classification

| Instrument or family | Status | Appropriate role | Important limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU AI Act | Binding EU regulation | Risk-based legal context, obligations, oversight, documentation, and implementation planning | Does not establish local clinical effectiveness or economic value |
| European Health Data Space | Binding EU regulation with phased implementation | Primary and secondary health-data access, exchange, interoperability, governance, and transition planning | Scope and operational dates must be verified at the time of use |
| EU HTA Regulation | Binding EU regulation | Joint clinical assessment context and national evidence pathways | Does not replace national economic, reimbursement, procurement, or local adoption decisions |
| ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 23894 | Published voluntary standards | Organizational AI management systems and risk management | Certification or conformity does not prove clinical or economic benefit |
| NIST AI RMF | Voluntary risk-management framework | Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage AI risk | Not a European legal instrument or healthcare-specific approval pathway |
| WHO AI ethics and governance guidance | International normative guidance | Public-interest ethics, rights, inclusion, accountability, and governance | Requires translation into local law, institutional controls, and evidence plans |
| NICE Evidence Standards Framework | National evidence guidance | Proportionate evidence planning for digital health technologies | Not a universal regulatory approval or economic reference case for every jurisdiction |
| TRIPOD+AI, STARD-AI, DECIDE-AI, SPIRIT-AI, CONSORT-AI, CHEERS-AI, PRISMA | Published reporting guidelines | Improve transparent reporting for defined study designs or stages | Reporting completeness is not equivalent to low risk of bias or valid methods |
| PROBAST+AI and GRADE-CERQual | Published appraisal approaches | Assess risk of bias/applicability or confidence in qualitative synthesis findings | Do not replace domain expertise, regulatory review, or local validation |
| CFIR, NASSS-CAT, RE-AIM/PRISM | Established implementation frameworks or models | Analyse determinants, complexity, outcomes, adaptation, scale, and sustainment | Selection should match the question; none is a complete AI governance system |
| TAM and UTAUT | Established acceptance theories | Examine perceived usefulness, effort, social influence, facilitating conditions, and intention | Individual acceptance does not establish safe implementation or sustained value |
| FAIR, FHIR, and OMOP | Data principles, interoperability standard, and common data model | Data stewardship, exchange, standardization, and reproducible evidence infrastructure | Technical conformance does not guarantee semantic validity, lawful use, or decision utility |
| AI-HEVG, AI-HED, TCAIO, institutional decision-state and transition logic, VALIDATE-AI, SHARE-AI | Author-developed or adapted decision aids proposed in the book | Integrate evidence, economics, governance, implementation, and lifecycle records | Not external standards, validated clinical instruments, or regulatory pathways |
