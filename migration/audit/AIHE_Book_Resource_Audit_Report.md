# Artificial Intelligence and Healthcare Economics
## Digital Resource Companion: Book-to-Resource Audit and Revision Specification

**Audit date:** 1 September 2026  
**Old book:** `AI_Healthcare_Economics_old.pdf`  
**Revised book:** `AI_Healthcare_Economics_Book_Oxford_English_LaTeX_Revised_sep_1_Back_Matter_Implemented.pdf`

## Executive determination

The companion requires a substantive revision rather than a simple replacement of image files. The revised book preserves the five-Part, fifteen-chapter architecture, but it changes the conceptual hierarchy within most chapters, reduces the number of principal figures and tables, changes the status of several author-developed frameworks, strengthens lifecycle decision provenance, and replaces the principal Chapter 15 applied case.

The old book contains **382 physical pages, 58 figures, and 62 tables**. The revised book contains **412 physical pages, 31 figures, and 46 tables**. The revised edition is therefore **30 pages longer while containing 27 fewer principal figures and 16 fewer principal tables**. The change reflects a deliberate movement from dense arrays of overlapping diagrams and tables towards more selective visuals, integrated narrative, and more explicit institutional decision logic.

## Principal implications for the companion

1. **Visual synchronization is a migration task.** The active figure registry must fall from 58 to 31. Old assets should not remain presented as current book figures. Useful retired visuals may remain in an archive or teaching-only layer, clearly labelled as companion extensions rather than reproductions of the revised book.

2. **Table-linked forms must be remapped.** Sixteen former tables are no longer part of the active book. Their useful content should be retained only where it adds practical value to a core form, workbook, guide, or worked example.

3. **Chapter 12 requires the largest revision.** The former emphasis on a package of levels, layers, gates, scoring anchors and instrument stacks has been replaced by decision identity, integration without aggregation, AI-HEVG, AI-HED decision provenance, institutional decision states, lifecycle transition triggers, version identity, material change, reauthorization, renewal, de-adoption, replacement and retirement.

4. **Chapter 15 requires a new principal case.** The old `AIHE-CASE15` compares edge, cloud or shared diagnostic AI. The revised book instead uses an **AI-assisted remote diagnostic pathway**. The old case may remain as an archived optional variant, but it should no longer be the primary Chapter 15 worked example.

5. **Page references are unstable.** All website, catalog and bundle references should use chapter/section labels and stable resource IDs rather than printed page numbers.

## Chapter-level resource consequences

### Chapter 1: AI as a Health-System Intervention

**Change in the book.** The chapter remains the conceptual foundation, but the revised edition removes three adjacent checklist/sequence tables and the threshold NMB figure. It places greater emphasis on option value, reversible adoption, and a single integrated managerial decision rule.

**Required companion action.** Update chapter titles only where older companion materials use 'AI as a Health-System Intervention'. Reconcile core records A01/A02 and CASE01 with the revised terminology; retire active links to old Figure 1.4 and Tables 1.4–1.6.

### Chapter 2: Institutional Readiness and Digital Foundations

**Change in the book.** The revised chapter is more explicitly use-case-specific. Broad digital-transformation and portfolio illustrations are reduced; the active visual set is limited to layered readiness and the data-governance lifecycle. The chapter culminates in a documented readiness decision rather than a maturity score.

**Required companion action.** Revise A03/A04, CASE02, chapter pages, and data-readiness forms so that critical dependencies, unresolved limitations, ownership, and the decision to proceed/remediate/pilot/defer are explicit. Remove active figure links to old Figures 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, and 2.6.

### Chapter 3: Human–AI Decision Systems and Managerial Action

**Change in the book.** The revised edition is organized around decision architecture, appropriate reliance, decision rights, meaningful oversight, collective sensemaking, and decision latency. The delegation spectrum and dashboard pathway are replaced by a more integrated decision architecture.

**Required companion action.** Retitle chapter metadata. Revise C04, CASE03/CASE03B and PY06/PY21/PY34/PY35 navigation to distinguish information, judgement, authority, action, feedback, and the closing decision window.

### Chapter 4: Productivity, Work Redesign, and Implementation

**Change in the book.** The revision distinguishes task efficiency, net labour saving, usable capacity, service productivity, bottlenecks, work redistribution, work intensification, and deliberate allocation of released capacity. Generic barrier/strategy material is reduced.

**Required companion action.** Retitle metadata. Revise B03, RG03, CASE04, PY22, PY23, and PY28. Add fields for fragmentation, downstream bottlenecks, work transfer, quality adjustment, capacity destination, and persistence.

### Chapter 5: Quality Management, Accreditation, and Organizational Learning

**Change in the book.** The revised chapter strengthens surveillance, signal recognition, escalation, incident investigation, CAPA, effectiveness verification, closure, institutional memory, and recurrence prevention.

**Required companion action.** Retitle metadata. Revise C01/C02, CASE05 and PY02 outputs so monitoring produces an assigned response, closure evidence, and a learning action. Replace old accreditation-to-learning and lifecycle diagrams with the revised SPC and learning figures.

### Chapter 6: Lifecycle Cost, Pricing, and Benefit Realization

**Change in the book.** The revision adds explicit distinctions among economic cost, cash expenditure, and accounting expense; budget incidence; financial recognition; NPV, ROI and payback; downside exposure; and renewal, renegotiation, and exit.

**Required companion action.** Substantively revise B01/B02/B03, CASE06, PY10, and PY11. Workbooks must separate cash-flow timing from economic resource use and must not recognize released capacity as a cash saving without a specified mechanism.

### Chapter 7: Economic Evaluation, Affordability, and Health Technology Assessment

**Change in the book.** The revised chapter expands the efficiency frontier, NMB, transferability, distribution, reporting, HTA translation, and the jurisdictional illustration of European joint assessment.

**Required companion action.** Spell out the chapter title in metadata. Revise B04, CASE07, PY12/PY13/PY16, and website text. Separate cost-effectiveness, budget impact, affordability, reimbursement, and local adoption.

### Chapter 8: Decision Modelling, Simulation, and the Value of Information

**Change in the book.** The revised chapter explicitly represents AI characteristics in models, classifies uncertainty, develops deterministic and probabilistic analysis, and gives greater weight to EVPI, EVSI, ENBS, research design, and model credibility.

**Required companion action.** Retitle metadata. Revise B05, RG05, CASE08, PY14/PY15/PY18/PY19/PY20. Add EVSI/ENBS and research-design outputs; ensure one-way sensitivity and VOI graphics match the new figures.

### Chapter 9: Problem-First Procurement and Vendor Governance

**Change in the book.** The revision recasts procurement around information asymmetry, incomplete contracts, continuing supplier disclosure, due diligence rights, upstream dependencies, post-award obligation registers, renewal as a new commercial decision, renegotiation, and credible exit.

**Required companion action.** Substantively revise D01/D05, CASE09, PY17/PY25 and contract resources. Replace one-time vendor evidence profiles with version-matched, continuing disclosure and obligation monitoring.

### Chapter 10: Governance, Accountability, and Institutional Risk

**Change in the book.** The revised chapter centres institutional authority, residual-risk acceptance, answerability, contestability, assurance independence, conflicts, dissent, legitimacy, reauthorization, and accountability beyond approval.

**Required companion action.** Revise C03/C04/C05, RG04, CASE10, PY07/PY08/PY26. Add residual-risk authority, dissent records, conflict declarations, reauthorization triggers, and legitimacy/communication evidence.

### Chapter 11: Evidence, Validation, and Real-World Evaluation

**Change in the book.** The revision separates internal validity, external/temporal transportability, local validity, decision utility, causal effect of use, and decision-grade synthesis. It adds estimands, reference-standard bias, evidence claims, and re-evaluation after material change.

**Required companion action.** Revise RG02/RG06, CASE11, PY04/PY05/PY24/PY30/PY31/PY32 and evidence records. Each output should state what the design can establish and what remains unresolved.

### Chapter 12: Decision Architecture and Lifecycle Governance

**Change in the book.** This is the largest conceptual change. The revised chapter reduces the former package of layered frameworks and gates in favour of decision identity, integration without aggregation, AI-HEVG, AI-HED decision provenance, institutional decision states, transition triggers, version identity, material change, reauthorization, renewal, de-adoption, and end of authorization.

**Required companion action.** Major revision required for D02–D05, RG01, CASE12, PY09/PY27/PY29, catalogs, process maps, and website. De-emphasize aggregate scoring and GATED-AI. Add a state-and-trigger model and a versioned decision history.

### Chapter 13: Circular Health Systems and Resource Stewardship

**Change in the book.** The revised chapter expands stocks and flows, value-retention pathways, inner and outer loops, circular leakage, physical and digital circularity, reverse flows, functional units, resource intensity, rebound, burden shifting, and geographical distribution.

**Required companion action.** Revise E01, CASE13, PY36/PY39 and dashboards. Add stock-flow accounting, loop classification, leakage, rebound and distributional fields.

### Chapter 14: Circular Procurement and Asset Stewardship

**Change in the book.** The revision replaces a simpler procurement/decommissioning treatment with supported service life, condition and dependency assessment, forms of obsolescence, eligibility before economic comparison, EAC, realizable residual value, decision thresholds, asset closure, and final destination.

**Required companion action.** Substantively revise E02/E03, CASE14 and PY37. Add an eligibility gate before EAC, supported-service-life assumptions, obsolescence categories, realizable rather than nominal residual value, and closure evidence.

### Chapter 15: Circularity-Adjusted Economic Evaluation and Sustainable Value

**Change in the book.** The revised chapter reconciles economic and environmental boundaries; distinguishes average and marginal consequences; strengthens provenance, monetisation limits, residual value, resilience, double-counting controls, incomplete monetisation, preference reversal, rebound, and spatial/intertemporal burden transfer. The applied case changes from an remote diagnostic pathway comparison to an AI-assisted remote diagnostic pathway.

**Required companion action.** Retitle metadata. Replace or archive CASE15 and revise E04/E05 and PY38 around the remote diagnostic pathway. Add common functional-unit checks, average-versus-marginal analysis, double-counting rules, burden-transfer reporting and disaggregated consequences.

### New examples recommended

1. **AIHE-CASE06B — Financial Recognition and Budget Incidence.**  
   This microcase should show why released capacity, avoided economic cost, cashable saving, revenue change and accounting treatment are not interchangeable.

2. **AIHE-CASE08B — EVSI and Expected Net Benefit of Sampling for a Staged Pilot.**

## Archive-access limitation in this run

The two PDFs were fully available through the document search and rendering layer. The two uploaded ZIP archives, however, were not exposed to the active filesystem and did not appear as extractable items in the file-search index. Consequently, this run could complete the book comparison and source-informed resource revision specification, but it could not perform byte-level extraction, in-place editing, validation and repackaging of the exact attached archives.

The final replacement ZIPs should be produced only from the exact supplied archives or from the corresponding connected GitHub repository. This avoids silently omitting files, overwriting newer resources, or presenting a partial reconstruction as a complete canonical release.
