Institutional decision stages

Ten iterative stages connect the four parts and twelve chapters. Use the smallest sufficient resource set and preserve separate evidence, judgement, authority, and safeguards.

Stage 1 · Chapters 1

Define the decision

Specify the service problem, population, complete intervention, realistic comparators, outcomes, value mechanism, stakeholders, and accountable authority.

Stage 2 · Chapters 2

Assess readiness and data

Evaluate institutional capability, data fitness, provenance, interoperability, lawful access, resilience, technical debt, workflow capacity, and prerequisite investment.

Stage 3 · Chapters 5, 11

Establish evidence and quality requirements

Define validation, reporting, appraisal, quality-control, accreditation, patient-safety, and independent-assurance requirements before consequential use.

Stage 4 · Chapters 6, 7

Model cost, value, affordability, and HTA

Estimate lifecycle cost, pricing exposure, benefit realization, cost-effectiveness, budget impact, affordability, distribution, reimbursement, and HTA implications.

Stage 5 · Chapters 8

Select the decision model and characterize uncertainty

Choose a decision or simulation structure that matches the causal and operational problem; document assumptions, verification, sensitivity, scenarios, thresholds, and value of information.

Stage 6 · Chapters 10

Assess governance and institutional risk

Evaluate accountability, human oversight, ethics, equity, privacy, cybersecurity, resilience, trust, contestability, communication, and institutional risk as connected requirements.

Stage 7 · Chapters 9

Procure and contract

Translate the defined service need into vendor evidence requirements, due diligence, acceptance testing, pricing, audit rights, update control, continuity, portability, remedies, and exit.

Stage 8 · Chapters 4, 12

Implement, monitor, and control change

Use staged implementation, role and competency design, post-deployment monitoring, algorithmovigilance, incident reconstruction, corrective action, material-change review, and renewal controls.

Stage 9 · Chapters 6, 9, 12

Reassess continuing value, affordability, and support

Reassess the current comparator, realized benefits, lifecycle costs, budget exposure, supplier support, infrastructure dependencies, continuity, residual value, and credible replacement or exit options.

Stage 10 · Chapters 12

Assemble the decision record and renew or retire

Integrate evidence, economics, governance, implementation, monitoring, conditions, dissent, and accountability into a versioned decision record and make an explicit continue, redesign, restrict, replace, or retire decision.
