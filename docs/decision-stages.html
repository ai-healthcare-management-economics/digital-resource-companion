Decision stages across the lifecycle

The stages are connected and iterative. Evidence discovered later may require revision of an earlier assumption or decision.

1. Frame

Problem, population, setting, comparator, decision authority and value hypothesis.

2. Establish readiness

Data, infrastructure, workflow, workforce, governance and service capability.

3. Appraise evidence

Technical, clinical, causal, implementation, economic and distributional evidence.

4. Model consequences

Costs, outcomes, uncertainty, affordability, service capacity and research value.

5. Procure and contract

Eligibility, supplier disclosure, pricing, assurance rights, portability and exit.

6. Decide and authorize

Current decision state, conditions, safeguards, unresolved uncertainty and review date.

7. Implement in stages

Retrospective assessment, silent evaluation, limited pilot, controlled scale and continuing review.

8. Monitor and respond

Signals, thresholds, incidents, corrective action, effectiveness verification and learning.

9. Reconsider after change

Material change, revalidation, reauthorization, restriction, renegotiation or replacement.

10. Renew or end authorization

Renewal as a new decision; continuity, retirement, asset closure and responsible disposition.
