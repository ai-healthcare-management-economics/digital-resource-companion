## Core institutional records

- **AIHE-A01 — Decision and Use-Case Specification** — Chapter 1: AI as a Health-System Intervention. Define the service problem, full intervention, realistic comparators, outcomes, value mechanism, decision rule, and review cycle.
- **AIHE-A02 — Stakeholders, Decision Rights, and Participation** — Chapter 1: AI as a Health-System Intervention. Identify affected parties, decision rights, participation duties, evidence contributions, and escalation authority.
- **AIHE-A03 — Institutional Readiness Assessment** — Chapter 2: Institutional Readiness and Digital Foundations. Assess strategic, data, infrastructure, workflow, workforce, quality, economic, governance, procurement, and sustainability readiness.
- **AIHE-A04 — Data Source, Provenance, and Fitness Record** — Chapter 2: Institutional Readiness and Digital Foundations. Document source and derived data assets, authority, definitions, transformations, quality, lineage, monitoring, and remediation.
- **AIHE-C04 — Human Oversight and Accountability Matrix** — Chapter 3: Human–AI Decision Systems and Managerial Action. Assign accountability, responsibility, consultation, information, escalation, pause, update, restart, and retirement authority.
- **AIHE-B03 — Benefit Realization and Capacity Conversion** — Chapter 4: Productivity, Work Redesign, and Implementation. Translate claimed benefits into measurable mechanisms, owners, baselines, conversion conditions, and realized results.
- **AIHE-C01 — AI Quality-Control and Accreditation Evidence Plan** — Chapter 5: Quality Management, Accreditation, and Organizational Learning. Integrate AI-enabled services into quality control, accreditation evidence, monitoring, thresholds, action, and closure.
- **AIHE-C02 — Corrective and Preventive Action Record** — Chapter 5: Quality Management, Accreditation, and Organizational Learning. Document nonconformity, containment, causal analysis, corrective and preventive action, effectiveness, residual risk, and closure.
- **AIHE-B01 — Economic Evaluation Specification** — Chapter 6: Lifecycle Cost, Pricing, and Benefit Realization. Define the economic decision, perspective, comparator, horizon, outcomes, costs, methods, uncertainty, safeguards, and validation.
- **AIHE-B02 — Lifecycle Cost and Pricing Record** — Chapter 6: Lifecycle Cost, Pricing, and Benefit Realization. Record acquisition through exit costs, resource use, pricing exposure, opportunity cost, residual value, and evidence.
- **AIHE-B04 — Cost-Effectiveness, Budget Impact, and HTA Record** — Chapter 7: Economic Evaluation, Affordability, and Health Technology Assessment. Summarize cost-effectiveness, affordability, distribution, HTA, reimbursement, adoption conditions, uncertainty, and recommendation.
- **AIHE-B05 — Model Selection, Uncertainty, and Reproducibility** — Chapter 8: Decision Modelling, Simulation, and the Value of Information. Select a decision model from the problem structure and document evidence, verification, uncertainty, and reproducibility.
- **AIHE-D01 — Vendor Evidence and Contract Requirements** — Chapter 9: Problem-First Procurement and Vendor Governance. Translate evidence, interoperability, data, security, monitoring, update, service, remedy, portability, and exit requirements into contract terms.
- **AIHE-C03 — Integrated Institutional-Risk Review** — Chapter 10: Governance, Accountability, and Institutional Risk. Review ethics, equity, privacy, cybersecurity, safety, continuity, trust, legal, and economic risks as one institutional system.
- **AIHE-C05 — Public Transparency and Communication Record** — Chapter 10: Governance, Accountability, and Institutional Risk. Define accurate, accessible communication about intended use, limitations, oversight, data, complaints, incidents, and alternatives.
- **AIHE-D02 — Lifecycle Decision State, Staged Evidence, and AI-HED Status** — Chapter 12: Decision Architecture and Lifecycle Governance. Control increasing commitment through problem, readiness, evidence, local validation, economics, pilot, scale, renewal, and retirement gates.
- **AIHE-D03 — Post-Deployment Monitoring and Algorithmovigilance Cadence** — Chapter 12: Decision Architecture and Lifecycle Governance. Define recurring and event-triggered surveillance across technical, workflow, safety, equity, economic, and contractual domains.
- **AIHE-D04 — Incident Classification and Reconstruction** — Chapter 12: Decision Architecture and Lifecycle Governance. Reconstruct technical, clinical, workflow, privacy, cybersecurity, equity, communication, and economic incidents consistently.
- **AIHE-D05 — Material Change, Reauthorization, Renewal, Replacement, and Retirement Record** — Chapter 12: Decision Architecture and Lifecycle Governance. Assess material changes and periodic renewal across system, data, workflow, vendor, price, law, evidence, alternatives, and exit.
- **AIHE-E01 — Circular-Economy and Sustainable-AI Assessment** — Chapter 13: Circular Health Systems and Resource Stewardship. Apply the circularity hierarchy from prevention and reduction through sharing, maintenance, reuse, refurbishment, recovery, and disposal.
- **AIHE-E02 — Circular Procurement and Supplier Scorecard** — Chapter 14: Circular Procurement and Asset Stewardship. Evaluate durability, modularity, repairability, interoperability, resource information, take-back, pricing, residual value, and exit.
- **AIHE-E03 — Supported Supported Asset-Life Decision Record** — Chapter 14: Circular Procurement and Asset Stewardship. Compare maintain, refurbish, replace, and shared-service options across suitability, life, cost, resources, resilience, and disposition.
- **AIHE-E04 — Circularity-Adjusted Evaluation Record** — Chapter 15: Circularity-Adjusted Economic Evaluation and Sustainable Value. Extend conventional economic evaluation with environmental, material, residual-value, resilience, equity, rebound, and burden evidence.
- **AIHE-E05 — Environmental, Circularity, and Disposition Dashboard** — Chapter 15: Circularity-Adjusted Economic Evaluation and Sustainable Value. Monitor acquisition, utilization, asset life, resource intensity, materials, take-back, recovery, rebound, resilience, and disposition.
