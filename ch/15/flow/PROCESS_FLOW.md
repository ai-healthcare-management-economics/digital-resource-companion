# Chapter 15: Circularity-Adjusted Economic Evaluation and Sustainable Value

Chapter-level decision process aligned to the fifteen-chapter reference edition.

## Process steps

### 1. Define the decision, functional unit, and system boundary
Specify the service output, population, quality level, volume, horizon, infrastructure, and lifecycle stages.

### 2. Estimate conventional outcomes and lifecycle cost
Report health, service, workforce, quality, and economic effects before circular adjustments.

### 3. Measure environmental and material flows
Quantify energy, water, carbon, virgin and recovered materials, waste, and critical dependencies per functional unit.

### 4. Estimate residual, recovered, and resilience value
Include remaining useful life, redeployment, take-back, recovery costs, continuity, and supply-chain effects.

### 5. Assess equity and burden distribution
Identify who benefits and who bears environmental, labor, access, and regional burdens.

### 6. Monetize cautiously or preserve disaggregated evidence
Document valuation sources, uncertainty, non-monetized effects, and double-counting checks.

### 7. Test uncertainty, rebound, and robust decisions
Compare options under plausible use, life, price, environmental, recovery, and induced-demand scenarios.

### 8. Issue a transparent decision with safeguards
Present conventional and circularity-adjusted results, mandatory constraints, conditions, and review triggers.

## Illustrative application

Edge, cloud, and shared diagnostic AI options are compared using conventional economics and environmental, residual-value, resilience, equity, and rebound evidence.
