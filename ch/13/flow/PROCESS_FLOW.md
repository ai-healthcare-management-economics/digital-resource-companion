# Chapter 13: Circular Health Systems and Resource Stewardship

Chapter-level decision process aligned to the fifteen-chapter reference edition.

## Process steps

### 1. Define the service need and functional output
Start with the health-system function rather than the asset or technology.

### 2. Prevent or avoid unnecessary demand
Eliminate duplicate, low-value, over-specified, or avoidable acquisition, storage, computation, and activity.

### 3. Reduce and optimize resource intensity
Improve utilization, model efficiency, storage, energy, water, material use, and maintenance.

### 4. Share and intensify safe use
Evaluate shared infrastructure, service models, capacity, access, resilience, and dependency.

### 5. Maintain, repair, reuse, and refurbish
Preserve safe useful life before replacement, recovery, or disposal.

### 6. Recover materials and dispose responsibly
Use verified take-back, chain of custody, recovery, secure erasure, and lawful final disposition.

### 7. Monitor rebound, burden shifting, and equity
Check whether lower unit burden increases total use or transfers burdens across populations or regions.

## Illustrative application

A health system applies the circularity hierarchy to compute, storage, sensors, and supporting hardware before approving new acquisition.
